History

At the end of October, Bumper had surgery to remove both of his eyes. He came through it well. In his foster home he was learning to find his way around in the dark, cone and all, and he was healing the way you hope a dog will.

On 8 November he started vomiting and stumbling. His bloodwork showed a sodium level of 198. A healthy dog sits around 145. At that level water is drawn out of the brain, and dogs can have seizures, collapse or die.

He was sent straight to an emergency hospital. His sodium can't be brought down quickly, because correcting it too fast causes the brain to swell, so it has to come down slowly, over days, with bloodwork checked through the day and night. The cause isn't known yet, and he needs more tests to find it.

K9 Responder has been quoted about $6,000 for his care. They took Bumper on knowing he would need more than most dogs. Paying his bill is the first thing this fundraiser does.

About

An emergency vet, one organisation at a time

Charlie is an Australian veterinarian who spent four years in emergency practice before moving to Los Angeles. She missed the work and wanted to keep making a difference, so she started Volunteer Vet Collective: each fundraiser, she picks an animal organisation, visits, films what they do, and raises money for them.

Her own income pays for travel and filming. Donations go to the organisations.

  1. Pick an organisation. Rescues, sanctuaries and response teams doing real work, at home and overseas.
  2. Visit and film. Showing what they do and what they need, on Instagram and TikTok.
  3. Raise and send. Donations are forwarded to the organisation and the receipt is posted here.

As featured in HuffPost, OK! and The Blast

Volunteer Vet Collective is being set up as a California nonprofit. Online donations open once its accounts are in place.
